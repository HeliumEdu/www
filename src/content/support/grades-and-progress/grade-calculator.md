## How to Open the Grade Calculator

  1. Open [**Grades**](https://app.heliumedu.com/grades)
  2. Click the **Grade Calculator** icon (calculator), to the right of the "Grade Trend" header
  3. If you have multiple classes, a list will appear — select the class you want to calculate for

The top of the dialog shows the class's grade, and its grade point when the class counts toward your GPA.

---

## Class Grade or Term GPA

Once the class's group has a [grade scale](/support/grades-and-progress/choosing-a-grade-scale/), use **Calculate for** to switch both tabs between **Class grade** and **Term GPA**. Term GPA also needs credits on the class; until it has them, the dialog says so. See [Tracking Your GPA](/support/grades-and-progress/tracking-your-gpa/) to add them.

---

## What Could I Get?

Projects your overall grade by letting you set hypothetical scores for every ungraded assignment.

Each ungraded assignment appears as a row with a slider and a percentage. Drag the slider to set a hypothetical score — your projected grade updates at the bottom of the dialog.

### Default Slider Positions

Each slider starts where your grades stand now: in a weighted class, its category's average, or your current class grade when nothing in the category is graded yet. In a class without category weights, every slider starts at your current class grade. Left untouched, the projected grade is your current grade. Before anything in the class is graded, sliders start at 100%.

### Projected Grade Indicator

The projected grade at the bottom is color-coded against your at-risk threshold:

  * **Green** — projected grade is at or above your at-risk threshold
  * **Yellow** — projected grade is below your at-risk threshold

In **Term GPA**, it adds the class's projected grade point and your projected term GPA, with the change from your current one. If the term counts toward **All** on the **Overall Grade** widget, your projected **All** shows too. It's green when your term GPA holds or improves, yellow when it drops.

### Reset

Click **Reset** to restore all sliders to their starting positions.

### How the Projection Works

For weighted classes, each category's hypothetical grade averages your existing graded points with the hypothetical scores, then applies the category's weight. For non-weighted classes, raw points are summed across all assignments. In weighted classes, categories with no **Weight** are excluded, since they do not count toward your grade.

## What Do I Need?

Calculates the exact score you need on a single remaining assignment to achieve your desired overall grade.

It assumes the rest of your ungraded work scores where your grades stand now, the same starting point as **What Could I Get?**. Once something in the class is graded, enter the score it gives you there and the projected grade lands on your target.

### Requirements

This tab unlocks when a category has **exactly one assignment left to grade**. If no categories meet this requirement, the tab shows a "Come back later" message. In weighted classes, categories with no **Weight** are excluded, since they do not count toward your grade.

For classes without category weights, the tab works from total points instead: it unlocks when the class has at least one ungraded assignment, and you select one of those assignments rather than a category.

### Steps

  1. Select the **target category** (the one containing the single remaining ungraded item), or the **assignment** for a class without category weights
  2. Enter your **Desired class grade (%)**
  3. Click **Calculate**

The result will tell you one of the following:

  * **You need X%** (and the points it takes) on that remaining assignment to reach your target
  * **You're already above your target** — your current grade already meets your target
  * **Score is unachievable** — the needed score exceeds 100%; adjust your target grade

### Planning for a Term GPA

In **Term GPA**, enter a **Desired term GPA** instead. Helium finds the lowest grade on this class's scale that gets your term GPA there, counting your other classes as they stand, and tells you the score you need, like "to reach B+ (87%+) in this class, for a term GPA of at least 3.40." If even the top grade falls short, it says so.
